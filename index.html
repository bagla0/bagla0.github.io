Akshat Bagla

Ph.D. candidate in Aerospace Engineering, Purdue University

I build open-source simulation software that predicts how composite structures, such as wind turbine blades, bend, buckle and carry load. I lead the development of OpenSG, which is listed in the U.S. Department of Energy's catalog of wind-energy software and used at three universities, and I build AI agents that run it. I work with Prof. Wenbin Yu and will graduate in May 2027. Before Purdue, I earned an M.Tech. at IIT Kanpur and designed drone structures at EndureAir Systems.

Research

Wind turbine blades

Real blades taper, twist and curve, which standard cross-section tools ignore. With Sandia National Laboratories and NREL, I built the path from a blade's design file to a validated beam model. On a 100-meter test blade it agrees within 1–2% with a 3‑D simulation that needed 2,000 CPUs for one to two days.

Buckling of thin blade skins

A blade's thin skin can suddenly fold under load, which is called buckling. I reuse the stresses from the beam model to check every segment of the blade, so a full blade is checked in 1.25 hours without modeling all of it in 3‑D. The results stay within 5% of analytical solutions and of Sandia's Sierra code.

Lightweight lattice panels

Panels with lattice cores are light and stiff, but too detailed to simulate cell by cell over a whole panel. I derived a plate model that replaces the detailed 3‑D simulation at 25 to 320 times lower cost, while matching its stresses and vibration frequencies within about 6%.

OpenSG

OpenSG works out how stiff a composite part is from the layers it is made of. You give it a detailed model of one small piece, such as a blade cross-section or a single repeating cell. It returns the properties of a fast beam, plate or solid model for the whole part and, afterwards, the stresses inside each layer. I built version 2 in JAX on the Air Force Research Laboratory's finite-element library, as part of an NSF project. It runs on GPUs, handles 25 million unknowns on one GPU, and gives exact derivatives of every result for design optimization.

Checked against VABS, SwiftComp, ANSYS and Abaqus · more than 200 automated tests · free under the Apache 2.0 license
