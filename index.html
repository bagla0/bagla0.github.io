OpenSG

Open-source multiscale modeling code I built
2023 – present

OpenSG is a general-purpose, GPU-enabled multiscale modeling code. It works out how stiff a composite structure is from what it is made of, and then the stress inside every layer.

It builds beam, plate, shell and solid models for parts as different as wind turbine blades and 3-D printed lattice panels, so engineers can run a fast model of the whole structure without losing the detail inside it.

I wrote version 1 in FEniCSx for a Sandia National Laboratories and U.S. Department of Energy project with NREL. For an NSF project with UT Arlington and the Air Force Research Laboratory, I rebuilt it in JAX to run on GPUs. Version 2 gives exact derivatives of every result for design optimization, and on large models it runs 5 times faster than the commercial code SwiftComp.

AI agents that run OpenSG

Purdue University, with AnalySwift
May 2026 – present

OpenSG now plugs into CompositesAI, AnalySwift's platform for composite design. Agents call it through skills I built and trained them with.

Each skill runs one step of an OpenSG analysis, from meshing a part to computing its stiffness and stresses, through tested scripts that do the same thing every time. Agents now run OpenSG from start to finish. They write its input files, run optimization studies, post-process the results and train machine learning models with its exact gradients. I also packaged CompositesAI as a Claude Code plugin.

One of my agent runs

TaskSwap the carbon spar caps of the 138 m IEA 22 MW wind blade for glass at four stations, and resize the glass caps so the blade stays as stiff in bending.

  1. Model the carbon baseline at four stations
  2. Resize the glass caps to the same bending stiffness
  3. Review CompositesAI's results
  4. Write the summary table and a recommendation

Result: bending stiffness held within 1% at all four stations, with the change in mass reported for each. Claude led the run, and CompositesAI did the analysis with my OpenSG skills.

A lattice sandwich plate, analyzed by agents

Claude planned the work and handed it out. CompositesAI built the lattice cell and used OpenSG to turn it into a plate model, Abaqus solved the plate, and OpenSG then recovered the 3-D stresses inside the lattice.

The stresses and vibration frequencies match a full 3-D simulation of the plate, and the whole run took 12 minutes instead of 5.4 hours.

New derivations

I also point agent teams at open research questions. Led by the CompositesAI agent, Claude subagents derived an exact solution for the stresses at the free edge of cross-ply laminates and an analytical formula for the through-thickness shear stiffness of general sandwich laminates.

Keeping agents reliable

  • Each agent gets one small, specific task.
  • Verifier agents check every result against physical limits, such as a stiffness that can never be negative.
  • A hook flags any agent that runs longer than 15 minutes and has it explain to me what it is doing.
  • When a skill built a wrong, unsymmetric laminate mesh, I traced the bug to the script its prompt called and fixed it.

In use

  • My skills are used at the University of Akron and in Purdue graduate structures courses.
  • Students in an AI/ML course used them for their research, and I updated the skills from their feedback.
  • When users asked to start from CAD models, I added a skill that goes from CAD geometry to stiffness, and I replaced static stress plots with clearer 3-D renders.
  • I gave workshops on CompositesAI agents at Purdue's Composites Manufacturing and Simulation Center and in AAE 590, and wrote 10 OpenSG tutorials and prompts.

Why a deck board wrinkles

Graduate Researcher, Purdue University
Industry project for Tangent Materials
Oct 2026 – present

This industry project asks why a layered composite deck board made by Tangent Materials wrinkles. I built an Abaqus model of the board and traced the wrinkling to three causes: layers that expand by different amounts when they heat up, the combined effect of heat and moisture, and failure of its glass fiber tape.

With Tangent's engineers, I worked out design changes to layer thickness, tape position, fiber direction and the order of the layers.

OpenSG on GPUs, and faster models

Lead Graduate Researcher, Purdue University
NSF project with The University of Texas at Arlington and the Air Force Research Laboratory
Oct 2025 – present

I lead the Purdue side of this project. I rebuilt OpenSG in JAX on the Air Force Research Laboratory's finite element library, so it runs on GPUs and gives exact derivatives. More about OpenSG

Lattice panels

Panels with lattice cores are light and stiff, but too detailed to simulate cell by cell over a whole panel. I derived a plate model that replaces the full 3-D simulation at 25 to 320 times lower cost, with in-plane stresses within 5% and vibration frequencies within about 6%. For 3-D shell lattices, a shell-based model I derived gets their solid properties within 5% of solid-element results, 4 times faster.

Thin walls

I derived two thin-walled beam theories that turn the outline of a section into beam properties, within 1% of a 2-D solid model on thin walls.

Wind turbine blades, from design file to stress

Lead Graduate Researcher, Purdue University
Sandia National Laboratories and U.S. Department of Energy project, with NREL
Jan 2023 – Sep 2025

Real blades taper, twist and curve, which standard cross-section tools ignore. I connected OpenSG, pyNuMAD and BeamDyn in one Python pipeline that takes a blade's design file to stiffness, loads and 3-D stresses, with my own mesh generators for tapered segments and airfoil sections. On a 100 m test blade, it agrees within 1–2% with a 3-D simulation that needed 2,000 CPUs for one to two days.

I derived a beam theory for curved, tapered thin-walled sections that is 10 times faster than PreComp and OptiStruct and more accurate.

To check for buckling without a full 3-D model, I reuse the beam model's stresses on each blade segment. A full blade is checked in 1.25 hours, within 5% of analytical solutions and Sandia's Sierra code.

Composite UAV structures

Structural Design Engineer
EndureAir Systems, Noida, India
Oct 2021 – Jul 2022

I built composite finite element models of tail-sitter and helicopter UAVs in ANSYS ACP. For a biplane quadrotor tail-sitter, I checked its sandwich composite wings under aerodynamic and thrust loads through take-off, transition, cruise and landing, and changed critical parts to save weight.

Working with the aerodynamics team, I ran vibration and dynamic analyses of the whole vehicle, and the results led to design changes that kept the structures clear of resonance. I also simulated the gear assembly of a mini helicopter as a multibody model in MSC Adams.

Composites made from chips

Graduate Research Assistant, M.Tech.
Indian Institute of Technology Kanpur
Aug 2019 – Aug 2021

For a project funded by India's Aeronautics Research and Development Board, I modeled composites made from randomly placed chips of chopped prepreg.

I improved the packing algorithm so the model packs chips at 59% instead of 32%. I wrote an Abaqus script that sets every chip's fiber direction automatically, a job my senior labmates used to spend hours on by hand. I also wrote a damage model in Fortran for Abaqus to predict the strength of these composites.
